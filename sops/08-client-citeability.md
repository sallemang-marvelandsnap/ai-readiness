# Writing pages that AI can quote

**For clients and their writers.** Marvel & Snap can make the site crawlable and structured. **You** still write the sentences that get quoted. This does not guarantee you will appear in ChatGPT or Google AI answers. It does mean that if they mention you, they have accurate sentences to quote.

Use this on **money pages**: home, product, service, “what we do,” and any page you would be unhappy to lose in search.

---

## Why this matters

People increasingly get answers from AI overviews, ChatGPT, and similar tools — often without clicking. Those systems prefer a **short, complete sentence near the top** of a page. If your definition is three screens down, or only in a hero image, they have nothing solid to lift.

---

## Do this

- **Start with the answer.** First paragraph: what it is, what you do or what this page is about, in one sentence, before the story.  
  *Yes:* “Northwind is construction software for industrial contractors.”  
  *No:* “In today’s fast-paced world, teams need better visibility…”
- **Make claims stand alone.** Someone should be able to quote one sentence with no other context.  
  *Yes:* “Licenses start at $199 per user per month.”  
  *No:* “It’s affordable, as we mentioned above.”
- **Use the same official name everywhere.** Pick “Northwind Track” or “NW Track,” not both.
- **Prefer specifics.** Numbers, dates, named customers, and your own research beat “industry-leading.”
- **Use headings that match real questions** when the section answers one.  
  *Yes:* “What is Northwind?”, “Northwind vs spreadsheets.”  
  *No:* “A new way to work”, “The Northwind difference.”
- **Put the important words in the page text**, not only on an image, icon, or animation.
- **If you include an FAQ**, use questions people actually ask, and answer them in full on that page (not “see above”). Skip an FAQ that is only there “for SEO.”
- **Refresh top pages a few times a year** (new example, current year). Stale pages get skipped.

---

## Skip

- Adjective stacks with no fact
- Asking us to “make a special AI file of every page” instead of fixing the copy — the live page is what gets read

---

## 30-second check before you send a draft

1. Read only the first three paragraphs. Could you paste them into an email as the definition?
2. Is that definition in the text, or only in the design?
3. Swap in a competitor’s name. If the sentence is still true, it may be too generic — are you able to add a fact that is uniquely yours?
