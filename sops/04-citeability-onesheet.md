# Citeability for writers (one sheet)

AI answers lift **early, complete, quotable** sentences. This is content craft, not keywords.

---

## Do this on money pages

- **Lead with the answer.** Paragraph one: what it is or what you do, in one sentence, before the story.
- **Self-contained claims.** “Northwind Track is construction progress-tracking software.” not “It’s the tool we mentioned earlier.”
- **One idea per short block** (two to four sentences). Lists and tables when the content is actually listable or comparable.
- **Question-style H2/H3** when the section answers a real question (“What is X?”, “X vs Y”).
- **Comparison and best-of** where honest: people ask AI that way.
- **Named sources, numbers, original data.** Case studies and proprietary stats get cited; adjectives do not.
- **Same name everywhere.** Pick “Northwind Track” or “NW Track,” not both.
- **Quarterly refresh** on top pages: new example, current year, resubmit in Search Console.

---

## Do not

- Bury the definition under three screens of atmosphere.
- Put the only explanation of the product in an image or a JS-only accordion.
- Write “affordable,” “industry-leading,” “as discussed above.”
- Add FAQ schema for questions that are not on the page.
- Expect `llms.txt` or schema alone to fix thin copy.
- Ask for a Markdown copy of every page so “LLMs can read it.” Google ignores those files; keep HTML as the source of truth. Curated `llms.txt` is enough.

---

## QA (30 seconds)

1. Read only the first three paragraphs. Could you quote them in an answer?
2. View source or reader mode: is that text actually in the HTML? Extra Bricks wrappers are fine; missing text (image-only / JS-only) is not.
3. Would a competitor’s name be clearer than ours in those sentences?

**Owner:** content. **Dev** only if the answer is trapped in the builder (image, widget, empty `post_content`).
